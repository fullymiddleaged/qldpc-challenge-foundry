# Research frontier scan, 2026-09-28

Question: which published methods could improve (a) our code search for the weight-6/8 2D-local bilayer cells at
n <= ~300 and (b) our exact-distance checks at d = 14-18? Only abstracts and landing pages were read, not full papers.
Claims below are what each source says. Lines starting "Us:" are our inference.

## Distance certification
- *SAT, MaxSAT, and SMT for QLDPC Distance Computation* (arXiv:2606.12445). Their abstract says scalability depends
  mostly on the cardinality constraints and optimisation bounds, not on parity reasoning. XOR-aware reasoning shows no
  systematic advantage, and branch-and-bound MaxSAT beats core-guided MaxSAT on hard instances.
  Us: our certify_sym uses CryptoMiniSat XOR clauses plus a seqcounter bound. Benchmark a totalizer or
  cardinality-network bound, and plain CNF parity, on the 140-6-14 bench before any more long runs.
- *Distance-Finding Algorithms for Quantum Codes and Circuits* (arXiv:2603.22532) and the codeDistance package
  (github.com/m-webster/codeDistancePYPI, `pip install codedistance`). They name connected-cluster (dist-m4ri
  `m4riCC`) and MIP (Gurobi/SCIP) as the fastest exact methods on the LDPC families they tested. They report
  QDistEvol as more accurate than QDistRnd for upper bounds. m4riCC needs dist-m4ri compiled (Cygwin on Windows).
  Us: the best candidate for an independent cross-check of any certify_sym PROVEN verdict (the 180s), and possibly
  faster outright. Try it on 140-6-14 and 240-12-12, which we have already proven, to compare times.
- *Quantum BB codes with weight-8 checks surpassing the BB benchmark* (arXiv:2609.06572) certify distances with a
  "cross-validated bit-mask verifier" and derive rigorous upper bounds from subgroup-coset kernel vectors.

## Code search
- Weight-8 BB (arXiv:2609.06572): the paper proves k = 2 dim R/(A,B) exactly. Its census includes [[144,6,>=15]],
  [[144,10,12]], [[144,16,10]] and [[72,14,8]]. Its abstract says nothing about layouts.
  Us: the formula gives cheap k screening. Laying out their codes in the weight-8 bilayer cell is a possible entry,
  but that raises the same attribution question as IBM's codes.
- Covering graphs (arXiv:2511.13560): an h-cover of a BB code has k_h >= k. For odd h, d_h <= h*d, and if k_h = k,
  also d <= d_h. Found [[64,14,8]] and [[144,14,14]].
  Us: lift our proven 2D-local codes (144/216/288) to covers. A cover of a torus layout keeps the same local
  structure, so the layout radius should carry over.
- LLM-guided evolutionary BB search (arXiv:2606.02418): about 200k candidates, a pipeline of MILP plus BLISS
  deduplication, and results including an indecomposable [[288,16,12]]. Us: BLISS canonical form would fix our known
  duplicate trap (signature() misses some equivalences).
- Coset 2BGA codes (arXiv:2606.17268, the family of the 180 codes) report weight-6 [[224,12,16]] and weight-8
  [[168,16,15]]. They are not 2D-local.
- 2D-local families: tile codes (arXiv:2504.09171) reach weight-6 [[288,8,12]] and weight-8 [[288,8,14]] with open
  boundaries. Strictly local tile-code SWAP routing is in arXiv:2607.05897. Planar open-boundary codes are in
  arXiv:2504.08887, and a nearest-neighbour-grid [[323,14,15]] is in arXiv:2606.19482.
  Us: our 2D-local bilayer entries ([[288,12,16]], [[288,8,20]]) already beat the tile-code parameters. The open
  question is whether open-boundary / tile ideas give single-layer (radius 4) entries.

## Follow-up, 28 Sep evening: DistQLDPC on the 180s
certify_sym on 180-18-14 stalled at --tlim 600 (open cubes fell to 32 at 70 cpu-h, then rose to 47; at length 16, 17
of 23 cubes timed out and split), so both 180 runs were stopped (status files kept, resumable).
2606.12445's own tables: MaxCDCL proved BB_144_14_14 in 3739 s, Gurobi in 2097 s. Totalizer vs seqcounter was close
(224 vs 218 solved), so the encoding is not the lever; branch-and-bound MaxSAT is. Its solver is DistQLDPC
(github.com/guluchen/DistQLDPC, GPL-3.0, commit 4498c28), built in WSL at ~/src/DistQLDPC.
- scripts/export_distqldpc.py poses one CSS side (Hz := I, Gx := zero row) and a certify_sym cube (zeros as Hx rows);
  scripts/distqldpc-one-z.patch adds -one-z=i,.. to force ones; scripts/run_distqldpc.sh runs prefixes in parallel.
- Calibration, one core each, all correct: 144-12-12 d=12 in 18.9 s (bundled two-sided form: 86.5 s),
  140-10-10 d=10 in 45.8 s, 140-6-14 d=14 in 119 s (certify_sym: about 250 cpu-s).
- 180 roots: root 1 (qubit 90 = 1, first orbit zero) gives 32 on 18-14 (4.8 s) and 16 on 20-14 (0.2 s). Root 0 and
  the unrestricted problem were still running at the time of writing (12 h cap); logs are in results/distqldpc/logs/.
  One open 18-14 cube of length 17 (leaf3) closed at 16 in 41 s.
- 180-20-14 root 0 (qubit 0 = 1): optimum 14 in 131 s. With root 1 at 16 and the checked X/Z duality, DistQLDPC
  gives d = 14 for 180-20-14 in about 2 cpu-minutes, where certify_sym had 139 open cubes after 75 cpu-h.
  The upper bound is a found weight-14 logical. The lower bound rests on MaxCDCL alone.
- 180-18-14 root 0: optimum 14 in 209 s (root 1: 32), so DistQLDPC gives d = 14 for 180-18-14 as well. Its four
  open certify_sym cubes tested (leaf0-3) closed at 15, 16, 15 and 16 in 41-149 s each.
- Unrestricted cross-check (no symmetry, no duality beyond the X side): 180-20-14 optimum 14 in 1161 s and
  180-18-14 optimum 14 in 1735 s, both matching the orbital roots.
- Next lead for a checkable lower bound: MaxCDCL with VeriPB proof logging, *Certified Branch-and-Bound MaxSAT
  Solving* (arXiv:2511.10273). A VeriPB proof would make a DistQLDPC optimum a certificate anyone can check.
Caveat: an optimum from DistQLDPC is a MaxSAT solver's claim with no proof certificate. It is an independent
cross-check of certify_sym, not a replacement for it.
